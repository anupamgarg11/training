Three runs, and Monday is rest

Tuesday is the hard session — tempo or intervals, labelled on the day. Thursday is easy. Saturday is the long run. Six training days, one rest day, exactly as you already train.

If a week only allows two runs, drop Thursday. Every time — it's the one whose absence costs least. If only one run happens, it's Saturday, always. And if two-run weeks stack three deep or more, stop growing the long run: hold it at the last distance you hit until you're back to three. Growing a long run on a shrinking base is how people get hurt in October.

On heart rate: the numbers are a guide, not a gate. If you can speak in full sentences, you're running easy — even if the watch says 165. Wrist sensors over-read badly during running, and Bengaluru humidity adds 10–20 bpm at the same pace. Don't walk just because the number climbed while your breathing stayed calm.

How to read the loads

Target loads step up every four weeks, but they're targets, not instructions. The real rule governs: when you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add 2.5 kg next session and drop back to the bottom of the range. If the table says 70 kg and you're grinding at 65, stay at 65 — the table is behind you, not ahead of you.

Log RPE on every working set. Target 7–9 on compounds, 8–10 on isolation. You have 1,424 logged sets and zero RPE entries, and that's the number that tells us whether the loads above are right.

The one exception: the side bend. Bodyweight, always, forever. Loaded lateral flexion is the one movement that can thicken the waist you're trying to narrow — and at 13% body fat your obliques will show from the Pallof press and the carry work anyway. Everything else on this page gets heavier over time; that one doesn't.

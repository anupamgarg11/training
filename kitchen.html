The Standing Order

Everything your kitchen needs to run without a daily decision. Section 1 is written to be handed straight to your cook — it's the only page they need.

1 · The cook brief

Standing instructions. Nothing here changes week to week, so it only has to be explained once.

  1. Chicken: cook 260 g raw per meal, twice a day Anupam's share works out to roughly 150 g per meal once it's split with his flatmate — that's the number that matters. Right now you're making 200 g and his share lands at 110 g, which is too little. Please weigh it raw; everything else can be by eye.
  2. Cooking method: air fryer, grill, or pan with 1 teaspoon of oil No deep frying. No cream, no malai, no butter-based gravies. Marinate with spices, curd, lemon, ginger-garlic — all of that is free and encouraged.
  3. Vegetables: two portions a day, 200 g raw weight each One with lunch, one with dinner. A mix is better than a single vegetable — 200 g total of any combination. Use only: broccoli, zucchini, capsicum, carrot, onion, tomato. Sautéed, steamed or air-fried, not boiled to death. This is currently the biggest gap in the diet.
  4. Carbohydrate: one portion per meal, weighed dry Basmati rice 75 g dry · pasta 80 g dry · 3 rotis (90 g Aashirvaad atta) · 4 slices high-protein bread. One of these per meal, not two. Section 2 lists the exact portion for each dish. On Fridays and Saturdays add 25% — those fuel the long run.
  5. Eggs: 3 boiled or an omelette, ready each morning Breakfast is currently being skipped and that has to stop. Keeping them ready removes the excuse.
  6. Skyr / curd: 50 g daily Plain, unsweetened. Small portion — it's for gut cultures, not protein.
  7. Fruit: two pieces a day Banana always available — it's the pre-run carb. Second piece can be anything.
  8. Tofu twice a week, replacing the chicken 230 g of firm tofu replaces 150 g of chicken — tofu has roughly half the protein per gram, so the weight has to go up. Currently planned for Thursday lunch and Sunday dinner. No fish, no paneer.

The one-line version, if the brief above is too much

260 g raw chicken per meal, 200 g mixed veg per meal, one weighed carb portion per meal, minimal oil, eggs ready in the morning. Get those five right and the numbers land within about 5% every single day, without anyone weighing anything else.

Why the portions went up rather than down

Cutting the mayo and butter out of the maths — which you never used — made your meals leaner, not richer. At your old portions they averaged 509 kcal against the 625 a main meal needs. Your cooking method is cleaner than the recipes it came from, and the carb portions never got adjusted to compensate.

You're right that ±100 kcal on any single meal is noise. The problem was that it ran the same direction every time: 116 kcal short, twice a day, every day. That's 230 a day, and it's most of the gap between the 1,900 you intend and the ~1,650 you were probably eating.

One consequence: you no longer need the daily protein scoop to hit target. These portions deliver ~150 g of protein from food alone. Keep the yeast protein for days you skip eggs or miss a meal — otherwise it just crowds out carbs you need for running.

Two notes on your method

Your skyr-and-sriracha sauce is genuinely excellent. About 48 kcal for 9 g of protein per serving, against ~135 kcal and zero protein for the mayo the original recipes use. Nothing to change — just keep using it everywhere.

The one recipe to modify: the fried rice reel uses chicken thighs, butter and oyster sauce. Use breast, avocado spray and your usual sauce and it comes in at 632 instead of the reel's 592-with-thighs — same calories, far more protein, much less fat.

5 · Eating out, and the rest

The plan has to survive a restaurant, because otherwise it survives about eleven days.

When you go out

  • Order protein first, then decide the rest. Grilled chicken, fish, tandoori, kebabs — anything not swimming in gravy.
  • One carb, not three. Rice or naan or fries. Pick the one you actually want.
  • Skip the starter, keep the dessert if you want dessert. Starters are usually fried and rarely memorable.
  • Don't compensate afterwards. One 2,600 kcal day inside a 1,900 plan costs you about two days of progress. Skipping meals the next day to "make up" costs you a week, because that's what starts the restrict-rebound cycle.
  • Just log it and move on. Photo, note it, next meal is normal.

Water, and the two habits

  • 3–3.5 L a day. You're at 2. Keep a 1 L bottle on your desk and finish it three times — far easier than counting glasses.
  • 500 ml on waking, before coffee. Gets you a sixth of the way before the day starts.
  • Fibre to 30 g comes almost entirely from the two large vegetable portions. This is the change most likely to visibly flatten your stomach inside two weeks.
  • Creatine 5 g daily — any time, with or without food. Consistency matters, timing doesn't.

What "tracking" actually means here

Do not weigh your food or log every meal in an app. You'll last two weeks. The whole system above is designed so that eating the default day correctly means you never have to count anything.

Two measurements only: the raw chicken (your cook does this), and your bodyweight, fasted, 3–4 mornings a week. The seven-day rolling average of that weight is the only feedback signal that matters — if it's falling 0.3–0.5 kg a week, everything above is working, whatever the app would have said.
